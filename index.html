01 / Healthcare AISpendRule · production work

When a contract changes, an invoice decision has to change with it.

Contract intelligence & invoice validation

Healthcare agreements hide pricing authority across scans, amendments, tables, sites, and effective dates. A fluent answer is useless if it cites the wrong term.

I architect the path from document intake to evidence-backed charge validation: versioned source records, semantic matching where judgment is needed, deterministic pricing and policy checks where correctness must be enforced, and review paths for ambiguity.

The design choice: make source authority and uncertainty explicit before making a financial claim.

Messy agreements→Versioned authority→Bounded decision→Human review when needed
02 / On-device AISafeScreen AI · 1st place, Qualcomm × Meta ExecuTorch Hackathon

A safety layer should not need your private screen sent to a server.

System-wide visual protection for Android

SafeScreen scans across apps and blurs explicit content on the device. The Android app requests no internet permission, so its privacy promise is visible in the architecture, not just the copy.

The team separated reliable primary blur behavior from a weaker deepfake signal, presenting the latter as an uncertain badge rather than an authoritative verdict.

The design choice: put privacy and model uncertainty into the product's actual behavior.

03 / Agent systemsARIA · 2nd place in a hackathon prize track

If an agent can take action, its constraints have to live outside the prompt.

Verifiable, paper-trading AI agent

ARIA explores autonomous trading with multiple strategies, paper execution, signed decision checkpoints, and verifiable rationale. The important question is not whether the agent can produce a trade idea; it is whether a risky idea can cross a hard boundary.

Risk gates are enforced in code, while accepted and rejected decisions leave a record that can be inspected later.

The design choice: separate model reasoning from permission to act.

Market signal→Agent proposal→Code-enforced risk gates→Signed record
04 / Knowledge interfacesChronoscope · active exploration

To search the history of ideas, first decide what counts as evidence.

A visual observatory for historical knowledge

Chronoscope explores visual search across historical encyclopedia illustrations. The experience invites people to follow how objects and concepts change over time, while the retrieval system must still identify the exact crop or text that supports a result.

Evaluation ties each judgment to the specific evidence a model saw. Human labels remain the ground truth; automated judges help review, but do not silently become it.

The design choice: design the evaluation unit alongside the interface, before claiming search quality.

Historical scans→Exact visual evidence→Human judgment→Explorable history

02 / How I think

The decisions behind the demos.

These are working principles I return to when the system becomes complex or the stakes rise.

01

Start with the decision.

Define what the user needs to decide, what evidence can support it, and what the system should say when that evidence is missing.

02

Give models a bounded role.

Let models interpret ambiguity. Put prices, permissions, risk limits, and other hard invariants in code and data that can be tested.

03

Design for the second day.

Version the sources, trace the path, make work resumable, and leave operators a safe way to inspect, replay, or correct a decision.

04

Measure the thing people actually see.

Evaluate the exact answer, passage, image, or intervention delivered to a user. A metric about a nearby artifact is not proof of quality.
